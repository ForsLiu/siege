## Open decisions (agent defaults, with item id)

All of the following are provisional bootstrap choices (item id `BOOT`). Every number lives in `data/dev/` and is a placeholder until SPEC.md defines the real one. Owner verdicts replace them.

- **BOOT-01 Stat vocabulary** (`src/sim/stats.ts`): `hp`, `attack`, `attackSpeed` (attacks/s), `armor`, `magicResist`, `range` (hexes), `maxMana`, `startMana`, `abilityPower`. `STAT_KIND`: `hp`, `attack`, `attackSpeed`, `abilityPower` are `mul` (accept flat and % modifiers); `armor`, `magicResist`, `range`, `maxMana`, `startMana` are `flat` (flat modifiers only; a % modifier on them fails schema validation). `range`, `maxMana`, `startMana` are rounded to integers after modifiers. Physical/magic mitigation = `dmg × K / (K + resist)` with `K = combat.mitigationConstant = 100`; `true` damage is unmitigated.
- **BOOT-02 Hex layout** (`data/board.json`, `src/sim/hex.ts`): odd-r offset coordinates (pointy-top hexes, odd rows shifted right), 7 columns × 8 rows, bottom 4 rows are the player half. Boards and encounters are authored in owner-half coordinates (rows 4–7); the right side of a fight is placed by point reflection through the board centre, which is an exact hex symmetry only for an even row count (schema enforces even rows). Neighbour order: east, then counter-clockwise; BFS ties resolve by discovery order.
- **BOOT-03 Tick model** (`data/dev/rules.json`): 30 Hz. Attack cooldown = `round(tickRate / attackSpeed)` ticks with attackSpeed clamped to [0.2, 5]; first attack fires immediately when in range. Movement: one hex per `moveSecondsPerHex = 0.5 s` along the BFS path to the nearest cell within range; when no route exists the unit steps toward the reachable cell closest to its target. Units act in uid order each tick (left side first; uids assigned by (row, col) of the authored position). A unit keeps its target until it dies; new targets are the nearest enemy by hex distance, ties to the lower uid.
- **BOOT-04 Fight end / timeout**: `maxSeconds = 30`; timeout = draw. A draw costs the player hp as a loss (`combat.drawCountsAsLoss = true`). Player hp loss on a lost round = `hpLoss.byRound[round-1]` (last entry repeats) + `perSurvivingUnit (1)` × surviving enemy units. Elimination on the same tick for both sides is a draw. P0-03 finalises this.
- **BOOT-05 Economy numbers** (`data/dev/rules.json` → `economy`): start 10 gold / 100 hp / level 1 / 0 xp; shop 5 slots; bench 9 slots; reroll 2 g; buy xp 4 g → 4 xp; +2 xp per round; max level 9; `xpToLevel[level]` = [0, 2, 6, 10, 20, 36, 56, 80, 100]; team cap = level. Income on `nextRound` = base 5 + interest (1 per 10 gold, max 5) + win bonus 1 + encounter reward gold. Sell = `floor(cost × mergeCopies^(star-1) × sellRefund(1))`; sold copies return to the pool. Pool sizes per cost tier 29/22/18/12/10; shop odds per level as in the file (rows sum to 100); when the rolled tier is exhausted the draw falls back to the next higher tier, then lower tiers. 3 copies merge (chained) up to 3 stars.
- **BOOT-06 Round loop**: `planning → combat (startCombat) → reward → planning (nextRound)`. The reward phase currently only holds the pending income (applied by `nextRound`); augment/item picks arrive with SPEC. All planning commands (buy/sell/place/bench/swap/reroll/levelUp) are legal only in planning. The run is **won only by beating the final encounter**; losing or drawing it with hp left is still a defeat (`endReason: defeat`). The run is lost at hp 0 or on `abandon`. Encounters are a fixed ordered list (`round` 1..n) — the `encounter` RNG stream is reserved for when SPEC introduces choice.
- **BOOT-07 Mana and casting**: +10 mana per attack landed by the attacker, +5 per hit taken; at full mana a unit casts at the start of its turn instead of attacking that tick, mana resets to 0. Stunned units skip their turn entirely (timers keep running). Bonus max hp from modifiers adds the delta to current hp; losing max hp clamps current hp.
- **BOOT-08 Effect vocabulary v0** (`src/sim/effects.ts`): `damage(kind, amount, scaling)`, `heal(amount, scaling)`, `statMod(stat, mode, value, duration|null)`, `stun(duration)`; targets `self | target | allies | enemies`; hooks `onCombatStart`, `onAttack`, `onHit`, `onCast`, `onKill`, `onDeath`. `scaling` adds `factor × caster stat`. Durations are seconds in data, converted with `max(1, round(seconds × tickRate))`. Stacking source ids: `ability:<unitId>` and `hook:<hookName>:<unitId>` (so one unit's repeated casts add as ranks, different units multiply). P0-01 extends this.
- **BOOT-09 Merge policy**: the kept copy is the lowest-uid copy on the board, else the lowest-uid bench copy; bench copies are consumed before board copies. Buying with a full bench is legal only when the purchase completes a merge.
- **BOOT-10 Bot RNG**: policies get their own `Rng` derived from `(seed, "policy:<name>")`, never a sim stream. `random` picks a command *type* uniformly among the legal types, then a command of that type (keeps `startCombat` reachable when hundreds of `place` variants are legal). Runs are abandoned after 5000 commands as a safety cap.
- **BOOT-11 Dev sample content**: 8 placeholder units (`dev.*`, costs 1–5, 3 stat blocks each, abilities exercising every v0 effect and hook), 10 encounters, 3 dev boards. Purely for engine tests; SPEC intake retires them. The hp-loss table was raised (3…20 per round) so a random bot dies around round 8, which keeps the bot-policy gates (P0-04) measurable.
- **BOOT-12 Content hash**: sha-256 over `name\n<canonical JSON>\n` for the four manifest files sorted by name; the `_provisional` comment keys are part of the hashed document.
- **BOOT-13 Fight seed**: each round draws a 32-bit fight seed from the `combat` stream. The fight itself currently consumes no randomness (all tie-breaks are stable); the seed is carried for effects that will need it (crits, spreads).
- **BOOT-14 Replays and hashes**: `RunState.hashes` = state hash (all fields except `hashes`) at run creation, after every `nextRound`, and at run end. A replay must reproduce every entry. Fight results are hashed over (winner, reason, ticks, events).
- **BOOT-16 In-tick resolution order (open for P0-03)**: units act sequentially in uid order within a tick (left side first), and damage, stuns and modifiers apply immediately. Pathing is mirror-invariant (team 1 enumerates directions point-reflected), but the sequential order gives the left side first strike, so a lethal board against its own mirror is not guaranteed to draw (a `.skip` test in tests/fight.test.ts documents it). Options for P0-03: (a) keep sequential resolution and rule the first-strike asymmetry in (player side acts first — an advantage for the player in PvE); (b) two-phase ticks: decide every unit's action from a start-of-tick snapshot, then apply in uid order, with contended move cells resolved symmetrically. Default until ruled: (a).
- **BOOT-15 Screens**: Title → Run → Results → Title plus a Dev fight screen; pause is a flag on Run/Dev fight; Results also allows starting a new run directly. Abandon = the `abandon` Command (ends the run as a loss with `endReason: abandon`).

### P0-01 — effect vocabulary v1

- **P0-01-01 Shields** (`src/sim/fight.ts`): `shield(amount, scaling?, duration)` grants a damage absorber. One instance per source id: a repeated grant from the same source refreshes it (the larger amount and the later expiry win) instead of stacking, so a re-cast cannot pile up absorbers. Absorption order is soonest expiry first, permanent last, ties by grant order. Shields sit between mitigation and hp, so they absorb `true` damage too. Death clears them. The ledger records `shielded` (granted) on the caster and `absorbed` on the wearer.
- **P0-01-02 Tags** (`applyTag(tag, duration)`): named state on a unit, one entry per (tag, source); re-applying keeps the later expiry. Tags are inert engine state today — they are emitted as events and readable by the renderer and tests; conditional effects that read tags arrive with the SPEC mechanic that needs them. Tag names use the unit-id character class.
- **P0-01-03 Auras** (`aura: { range, effects }` on a unit): effects are restricted to `statMod` and `applyTag` with `duration: null`, because the aura itself is what starts and ends them. Once per tick, before any unit acts, each aura effect is kept applied to exactly the units within `range` hexes that its own target selector picks, under the source id `aura:<defId>:<uid>:<index>`; entering the radius applies it once, leaving it (or the owner dying) removes it. Nothing is re-applied while it is already up, so per-tick upkeep can never stack. Damage/heal auras would need a period and are not in v1.
- **P0-01-04 Hook chain depth**: hooks can fire hooks (`onTakeDamage` → damage → `onTakeDamage`). The chain is cut at a fixed depth of 8 nested hook firings — deterministic, no wall-clock, and a data loop can never hang the sim. Effects at the cut-off simply do not run.
- **P0-01-05 `onRoundStart`**: fires once per fight, for every unit in uid order, before any `onCombatStart` hook, so data can separate pre-combat setup from combat-start effects. There is no in-fight notion of a round; a run-level round trigger arrives with the SPEC mechanic that needs it.
- **P0-01-06 Projectiles** (`spawnProjectile(ref)` + a `projectiles` registry in the units file): travel ticks are fixed at spawn from the distance then — `max(1, round(distance / speed × tickRate))` — so a moving target does not change the arrival tick. Projectiles resolve at the start of a tick in spawn order, before unit turns; they fizzle (no effects) when the target is dead on arrival, and still resolve when the *owner* has died, with the owner as the effect source. A projectile may not reference itself. P0-02 adds travel shapes (radius / line / nearest-k) and the renderer layer.
- **P0-01-07 Damage pipeline**: every point of damage walks `preMitigation → mitigation → shield → post` in that order. `preMitigation` clamps the raw amount to ≥ 0 and is the seam for amp/reduction effects; `mitigation` is `K/(K+resist)` by kind (`true` skips it); `shield` consumes absorbers; `post` applies hp, mana, ledger, the hit event, then `onTakeDamage` and the death check. The hit event carries `raw`, `mitigated`, `absorbed` and the applied `amount` so the ledger can be checked against the events.
- **P0-01-08 Ledger**: per unit `dealt` (hp removed from *others*; self-damage is not credited), `taken`, `healed`, `shielded`, `absorbed`, `mitigated`, `kills`, `deaths`. `dealt`/`taken` equal the sum of the applied `amount` over the hit events, which is what P0-03 checks.
- **P0-01-10 Max-hp changes are proportional**: when a modifier changes a unit's max hp, current hp scales with it (`hp × newMax / oldMax`), replacing BOOT-07's "add the delta on grant, clamp on expiry". That rule was not idempotent, so a repeating +hp modifier (or an aura toggling on the radius boundary) was a net heal — a unit could out-heal all incoming damage. Proportional scaling makes grant/remove a no-op on a full-hp unit, which is the case BOOT-07 was written for.
- **P0-01-11 Zero max hp kills**: a modifier that drives max hp to 0 kills the unit on the spot (killer = itself) instead of leaving it alive at 0/0 hp and counted as a survivor.
- **P0-01-12 Stat saturation**: `computeStat` saturates at `STAT_MAX` (1e9, the same bound the schemas enforce on authored values) instead of throwing when enough multiplicative sources compound. A content overflow must never throw out of `fight()`.
- **P0-01-13 Projectile cycles**: content validation rejects any projectile spawn cycle, not just direct self-reference — a cycle made projectiles in flight double every hop and hung the fight.
- **P0-01-09 Dev content**: `dev.warden` (shield, applyTag, aura, `onRoundStart`, `onTakeDamage`) and `dev.arbalest` (spawnProjectile, `onAttack`, `onCast`) were added so the dev set exercises every effect type and every trigger. Both are on the final dev encounter and on the new `data/dev/boards/c.json`, so the tools and the run-level tests actually execute the v1 vocabulary rather than only carrying it in JSON. Provisional like the rest of `data/dev/`.

### P0-B1 — erasable TypeScript only

- **P0-B1-01**: the whole codebase (`src`, `tools`, `tests`) is restricted to *erasable* TypeScript — no constructor parameter properties, enums, namespaces or `import x = require`. The sweep spawns `tools/sweep-worker.ts` as a worker thread loaded by Node's own type stripping (Node >= 22.18), which rejects non-erasable syntax, and a `--import tsx` preload is not a fix: it resolves tsx against the process CWD and does not transform the worker's graph. Enforced by `erasableSyntaxOnly` in tsconfig (`npm run check`) and by a token scan in `tests/architecture.test.ts` (the fast tier, which is the per-item gate). `package.json` engines is `>=22.18` accordingly.

### P0-15 — `dev:` command namespace

- **P0-15-01 Where the gate lives**: `RunConfig.devCommands` (set by `createRun(seed, content, { devCommands })`) decides whether the sim accepts a `dev:` command; `src/app/main.ts` passes `__SIEGE_DEV__`, which `vite build` defines as `false`, so a production build rejects every dev command with `dev commands are disabled in this run (<type>)`. The gate is checked before the phase checks so the reason never depends on the phase. A replay must be given the same flag (`replayRun(..., { devCommands: true })`), which is why `RunLog` now carries it.
- **P0-15-02 Phase**: every `dev:` command requires the planning phase, like the ordinary economy commands. Toggling invincibility mid-combat would mean mutating a fight that is already resolved (combat resolves inside `startCombat`), so there is nothing to toggle into.
- **P0-15-03 Augment and item ids are free-form for now**: `dev:addAugment` and `dev:giveItem` validate only the shape of the id (non-empty, <= 64 chars) and append it to `state.augments` / `state.itemBench`, because no augment or item content exists yet. P0-24 (augments) and P0-27 (items) replace the shape check with a lookup in their content tables and own those state lists; the commands and the command log do not change.
- **P0-15-04 Spawned units and the pool**: `dev:spawnUnit` takes `mergeCopies^(star-1)` copies out of the shared pool when they are there and silently takes fewer when they are not (`takeFromPool`), so a cheat can conjure a unit the pool has run out of without ever driving a pool count negative. The other side of that is capped too: `returnToPool` clamps at the unit's configured `poolSize`, and `checkInvariants` now flags a count above it — otherwise spawning past the pool and selling the copies back would inflate the pool above its data-defined size and skew shop odds for the rest of the run (code review on P0-15). Pool reads go through `poolCount` (own-property only), so an id inherited from `Object.prototype` cannot poison the pool. Spawned copies merge exactly like bought ones. Board spawns still respect the team-size cap and the player half; those are invariants, not economy.
- **P0-15-05 `dev:skipRound` records a round with no fight**: `RoundRecord.fight` is now `FightSummary | null` with a `skipped` flag, instead of a fabricated "win" summary that would pollute sweeps and run reports. The `combat` rng stream is untouched by a skip. The reward is the normal one for a win (`computeReward(..., won = true)`), and skipping the final encounter wins the run.
- **P0-15-06 Invincibility is a damage clamp**: `dev:invinciblePieces` passes `FightRules.invincible = { left: true, right: false }` into `fight()`, which clamps the *applied* part of each damage instance so the unit stops at 1 hp. The ledger and the `hit` event report the clamped amount, because both measure hp actually removed (P0-03 asks the ledger totals to equal the sum of the hit events); the unclamped amount is kept only to keep `onTakeDamage` firing, so the cheat changes nothing but the dying. It is an argument, so fights stay pure and a cheated run replays hash for hash. A `maxHp` modifier that drives max hp to 0 still kills the unit — invincibility protects against damage only. `dev:invinciblePlayer` is separate and only zeroes the round's hp loss.
- **P0-15-08 Cheated gold has a cap**: `dev:gold` refuses any amount that would take gold above `MAX_DEV_GOLD = 2 ** 40` (or below 0). Without an upper bound, two `1e308` top-ups made gold `Infinity`, which `canonicalJson` cannot serialise — every later round-boundary hash threw and the run became unhashable and unreplayable (code review on P0-15, Major). The cap is an input bound in `/src`, not a tuning number: it leaves ~13 orders of magnitude of headroom, so ordinary round income can never push gold out of the safe integer range.
- **P0-15-09 A `RunLog` replays itself**: `RunLog` carries `devCommands` and `replayLog(log, content)` feeds it into `replayRun`, so the recorded flag cannot silently disagree with how the replay is run (a disagreement mismatches at hash index 0, because `config.devCommands` is inside the state hash).
- **P0-15-07 Bots never cheat**: `legalCommands` deliberately excludes the `dev:` namespace, so policies, sweeps and fuzz runs cannot reach it.

### P0-18 — Battle sandbox screen

- **P0-18-01 Placement is roster-button, not click/drag**: unlike the run screen's board (P0-09), adding a unit to a sandbox side is a single "+L"/"+R" button per roster entry that auto-places it on the next free cell of that side's owner-half, scanned row-major from the board's top player row; there is no click-to-place or drag-and-drop onto specific hexes. The sandbox screen is a numbers tool for testing matchups and stat overrides, not a placement-accuracy exercise, and the exact cell rarely matters to the questions it answers (win rate, damage dealt); reusing the run board's drag machinery for a second, differently-shaped setup (two independently-authored owner-half sides instead of one board plus an encounter preview) was not worth the item's scope. `col`/`row` are still real `SandboxUnit` fields, so a saved setup can be hand-edited for exact placement if it ever matters.
- **P0-18-02 Saving a sandbox setup must not reload the page**: `src/data/browser.ts` loads all of `/data` through `import.meta.glob('../../data/**/*.json', { eager: true })`, and Vite's dev server treats a *new* file matching that glob as a reason to fully reload the page (there is no HMR acceptor for glob membership changes). The dev data endpoint's POST handler (`tools/vite-dev-data-plugin.ts`) writes a brand-new `data/dev/boards/sandbox-<name>.json` on every first save of a setup, which without a fix wiped the in-progress sandbox editor (and any other screen's state) the instant "Save" was clicked — found by driving the built screen in a real browser, not by the unit tests, since vitest never runs Vite's dev server. Fixed by excluding `**/data/dev/boards/sandbox-*.json` from `server.watch` in `vite.config.ts`: these files are dev-authored test fixtures the sandbox screen loads on demand, not game content a running app needs to hot-reload on, unlike the future Tuner's (P0-07) edits to existing content files, which is the one case this project actually wants that reload for.

### P0-19 — Unit inspector model

- **P0-19-01 `traits` is always empty**: the item text asks for "traits with a one-line effect each" and modifier sources including "trait", but no trait system exists yet (P0-25). `InspectorModel.traits` is typed and present so the panel (P0-20) and P0-25 have a stable field to fill in, and stays `[]` until then.
- **P0-19-02 Signature takes `content` too**: the item text writes `inspectorModel(state, uid)`, but every other view-model function in this codebase that needs a unit's data also takes `content` (`sellValue`, `xpNeeded`, `currentEncounter`, ...), because `RunState` stores only `defId`/`star`/`items`, not the `UnitDef` itself. `inspectorModel(state, uid, content, combat?)` follows that convention; `content` is the missing third argument, not a design choice.
- **P0-19-03 Combat-live values are an optional caller-supplied overlay, not derived from `RunState`**: `RunState` keeps only a `FightSummary` (aggregate numbers) after a fight, never a live per-unit snapshot — the actual live hp/mana/shields/modifiers exist only inside the sim's `FightUnit` during `fight()`, or in the renderer's replayed `UnitSnapshot` timeline (`src/render/timeline.ts`) during playback. So `inspectorModel` takes an optional `CombatOverlay` (hp/maxHp/mana/maxMana/shields/modifiers) the caller reads off whichever of those it has; passing `null` (the default) gives the planning-phase view (mana = min(startMana, maxMana), no modifiers, `combat: null`). This keeps the model itself sim-independent and testable with plain data, per "selection lives outside sim state."
- **P0-19-04 Ability text is a generic effect-vocabulary renderer, not authored copy**: no unit in `data/dev` carries hand-written ability text (SPEC.md doesn't exist yet), so `describeEffect` in `src/app/inspectorModel.ts` turns each `Effect` into one clause ("Deal 40 magic damage to the target", "+50% attack to itself for 4s", ...) with any `scaling` resolved against the unit's current stats, and joins an ability's effects with "; ". This is mechanism, not content: it reads only the existing effect vocabulary and produces no new tuning numbers.

### P0-09 — UI shell hardening

- **P0-09-01 The play area is 16:9, so a narrow window letterboxes vertically**: `letterbox()` centres a 16:9 box in the canvas and the hex grid is fitted inside that box, per the item text. The grid's own aspect is about 1:1, so in a window narrower than 16:9 the board is smaller than it would be if it used the whole canvas (the space becomes top/bottom bars). That is the point of resolution independence — the same window shape shows the same layout on every machine — and it is the shape the sandbox and HUD screens (P0-17…P0-23) will be designed against. If playtesting says the bars cost too much, the alternative is to letterbox to the grid's aspect instead, which is a one-line change in `src/render/layout.ts`.
- **P0-09-02 Click and drag are separate gestures**: a press that never moves is a click (select / bench / swap / place, exactly as before drag existed, decided by `boardClick`); a press that moves is a drag whose drop is decided by `boardDrop`, and the trailing `click` event is suppressed. Neither path changes the selection on mousedown, because the bench card that receives the mousedown would be rebuilt out from under the gesture (QA on P0-09).
- **P0-09-03 Hotkeys are not pre-gated by phase**: `hotkeyAction` maps keys to actions and only the view actions (`speed`, `pause`) are handled locally; every other action becomes a Command and the sim decides, so a mistimed key reports the sim's reason in the HUD instead of doing nothing silently. Speed and pause work during combat playback, which is when they matter.
- **P0-09-04 Abandon during a run-ending fight**: `RunController.abandon()` drops the playback, and when the fight had already ended the run it calls the finish path itself. Dispatching `abandon` there would be rejected with "run has ended" and the results screen would never appear — the soft-lock QA found on P0-09.

### P0-20 — Inspector panel & board readability

- **P0-20-01 Renamed `cellsWithin` to `hexesWithin`**: the item's acceptance text names the function `hexesWithin(origin, range)` directly; `src/sim/hex.ts`'s existing disc helper (`cellsWithin`, P0-01) had exactly that signature and only two callers (itself and its test), so it was renamed rather than adding a duplicate alias.
- **P0-20-02 "Ring" means the disc (`hexesWithin`), not the exact-radius ring**: the feedback text calls the highlight a "hex ring", but the acceptance criterion pins its cells to `hexesWithin(origin, range)` (inclusive disc), not `ring()` (exact radius only, P0-01). The acceptance text is normative per CLAUDE.md's sources-of-truth order, so the highlighted area is every hex the unit can reach, not just its outer edge.
- **P0-20-03 Ability range = aura range; no other ability shape carries a range**: the effect vocabulary (`src/sim/effects.ts`) has no range field on ordinary `UnitAbility` effects, only on `AuraDef.range` (a continuous passive). `rangeRings()` therefore treats "the ability range too if the ability has one" as the unit's `aura.range` when it has an aura, and omits the ability ring otherwise. If SPEC.md later gives a targeted ability its own range, this is the function to extend.
- **P0-20-04 Shop cards preview on hover, not click**: "click any piece... to inspect it" conflicts with the shop card's existing, separately-tested click-to-buy (P0-15/bootstrap). Buying is the primary, established interaction; inspecting a shop offer previews it on `mouseenter`/`mousemove` instead (`shopPreviewModel`, tier 1, no items, no combat overlay), delegated to the stable `.shop` container rather than the per-card buttons — those are rebuilt by `replaceChildren()` on every buy/reroll, and a card removed out from under the pointer never fires its own `mouseleave`, which would otherwise leave the shop preview stuck open (found live-testing this screen with Playwright).
- **P0-20-05 Board/bench selection doubles as the inspector's subject**: `RunController.selectedUid` (already the placement/sell selection, P0-09) is reused as the inspector's subject for board and bench units rather than adding a second selection field, per the designer note ("a selection field that lives outside sim state") and because the two concepts were never going to disagree (the placement selection is exactly "the piece the player is currently focused on"). `SandboxController` gets its own `selected: {side, index}` field, since sandbox rows have no placement-selection concept to reuse and no `uid`.
- **P0-20-06 Combat-time live inspection is deferred**: the feedback text asks for live hp/mana/shields while a fight is playing, but board clicks are already disabled during combat playback (P0-09, unrelated to this item) and mapping a click to a `FightUnit`/`UnitSnapshot` mid-interpolation is a separate, non-trivial piece of work with no acceptance criterion in this item pinning it down. The inspector and range ring are planning-phase only for now (`rangeRing` is omitted from `drawFight`/playback calls); a future item can wire combat selection through the same `InspectorModel`/`CombatOverlay` machinery P0-19 already built for it.
- **P0-20-07 `InspectorModel.uid` is now nullable**: shop and sandbox previews are not owned units and have no `uid`. Widening `uid: number | null` (it had no consumer besides tests before this item, per P0-19-… above) was cheaper and clearer than a parallel "preview model" type duplicating every other field.
- **P0-20-08 Escape closes the inspector instead of pausing on that press (code review)**: when a unit is selected/inspected during a live run, the first Escape only closes the inspector; pause (the existing, spec'd Escape mapping) needs a second press once nothing is left to inspect. A focused text/number input keeps its own Escape ahead of this carve-out (unchanged from before P0-20, since the whole hotkey block already skips `HTMLInputElement` targets).

### P0-21 — Income preview, gold panel & round-end summary

- **P0-21-01 No win/loss-streak mechanic exists; `streakBonus` is always 0**: `EconomyRules` (`src/sim/rules.ts`) has a flat `winBonus`, not a streak rule, and `RunState` tracks no running streak counter. The feedback text itself hedges ("win/loss streak bonus... if the rules define one") and BACKLOG.md matches ("where the rules define one"), so `incomePreview`'s `streakBonus` field is always 0 today — present in the shape so a future streak rule in `data/dev/rules.json` only needs `incomePreview` to stop hard-coding it to 0, with no breaking change to callers. No new mechanic was invented for this item.
- **P0-21-02 `incomePreview`'s win bonus is 0 before combat resolves**: the gold panel is shown during planning, before the round's fight happens, so whether the round is won is not yet known. `incomePreview` returns `winBonus: 0` pre-combat (a guaranteed-minimum preview) and the UI separately notes the rule's flat amount ("+1 more on a win", read straight from `content.rules.economy.winBonus`, not added into the total) so the player still sees what's at stake. In the `reward` phase (after combat, before `nextRound`), `incomePreview` returns the exact `state.pendingReward` breakdown instead, which is what the acceptance criterion ("equals the gold actually granted at nextRound") is anchored to.
- **P0-21-03 The round-end summary shows the next encounter's id, not its type**: the item's raw text asks for "the next round's encounter type", but the `Encounter` schema has no `type` field — that's explicitly P0-22's job ("the encounter schema carries `type` and a reward preview"), the very next backlog item. Showing `encounter.id` (e.g. `dev.e02`) is the closest available stand-in; P0-22 should swap this line to the real type once it lands.
- **P0-21-04 The gold panel (planning) and the round-end summary (reward) share `incomePreview`, not identical wording**: feedback §2 says "the same breakdown appears in the round-end reward summary" — read as the same *numbers* (both call `incomePreview(state, content)` and neither computes a gold amount itself), not necessarily the same rendering. The panel is one compact line (`incomeLine`); the summary is itemized ("rewards line by line", per §5's own wording) via a separate `summaryLines` formatter. Both are pure display code in `src/ui/runUi.ts` — no arithmetic on gold amounts happens there, only string formatting and reading rule constants (`eco.interest.per/max`, `eco.winBonus`, `eco.xpPerRound`) verbatim for labels.

### P0-22 — Round track

- **P0-22-01 `type` is a display label today, not a new mechanic**: `Encounter.type` (`normal | elite | boss | treasure | augment`, `ENCOUNTER_TYPES` in `src/sim/rules.ts`) drives only the round-track icon and the `augmentOffer` flag in its reward preview; it does not change fight difficulty, loot, or anything else the sim does. Elite/boss/treasure/augment rounds fight and reward exactly like a normal round of the same `reward.gold` until a later item (P0-24 augments, P0-28 loot) gives the type actual behavior. `data/dev/encounters.json`'s 10 rounds are assigned a mix of all five types (rounds 3/7 elite, 5 treasure, 8 augment, 10 boss, the rest normal) purely to exercise every icon in the dev build; this is provisional sample content like the rest of `data/dev`, not a difficulty curve.
- **P0-22-02 Reward-preview `items`/`augmentOffer` are placeholders**: the item text asks the preview to include "item drops, augment offer", but no loot-table or augment-offer content exists yet (P0-24/P0-28). `RoundTrackRewardPreview.items` stays `[]` (matching `InspectorModel.traits`'s P0-19-01 precedent) and `augmentOffer` is a bool derived purely from `type === 'augment'` (true today only for round 8, with no actual offer behind it) — a stable field for those later items to fill in, not invented content.
- **P0-22-03 Hover uses the native `title` tooltip, not a custom overlay**: "hovering a round shows its reward preview" is satisfied with a plain DOM `title` attribute (`trackTooltip` in `src/ui/runUi.ts`) rather than a styled popup like the P0-20 inspector panel — proportionate to what this item asks for (a preview string), and avoids a second hover-delegation mechanism so soon after P0-20's shop-card one. A richer hover panel can replace this later without changing `roundTrack`'s data shape.
